▪Natural-History Board Map

  • DPrepathogenesis: risk/determinant interaction but no disease → primordial/primary prevention.
  • DBiologic onset: disease begins before symptoms → secondary-prevention opportunity if detectable.
  • DSubclinical disease: pathology present, symptoms absent → screening/case finding.
  • Clinical horizon: disease becomes clinically recognizable.
  • Clinical disease: diagnosis/treatment; prevention level depends on objective and disease stage.
  • DLate disease: chronicity, complication, disability → tertiary prevention.
  • Board traps: lead-time bias and length bias can make screening appear beneficial without reducing actual mortality.

▪Ten Outbreak-Investigation Steps

  1. Prepare for field work.
  2. Establish the existence of an outbreak.
  3. Verify the diagnosis.
  4. Define and identify cases.
  5. Describe by time, place, and person.
  6. Develop hypotheses.
  7. Evaluate hypotheses analytically.
  8. Refine hypotheses / conduct additional studies.
  9. Implement control and prevention measures.
  10. Communicate findings and continue surveillance.
  • CDC identifies public-health surveillance and steps of outbreak investigation as core epidemiologic competencies.
  • Control can begin before Step 9 when delay would cause preventable harm.

▪Epidemic Threshold

  • Outbreak/epidemic = observed occurrence greater than expected occurrence for the specified population, place, and period.
  • Use the disease-specific DOH alert/epidemic threshold where defined.
  • [SUPPLEMENTARY illustrative rule only]: historical mean + 2 SD.
  • DExample: mean weekly count = 10; SD = 3 → illustrative threshold = 16 cases/week.
  • One case may be exceptional enough for immediate investigation if the disease is ordinarily absent/eliminated.

▪Ten Steps

  1. Prepare for field work.
  2. Establish existence of an outbreak.
  3. Verify the diagnosis.
  4. Define and identify cases.
  5. Describe by time, place, person.
  6. Develop hypotheses.
  7. Evaluate hypotheses analytically.
  8. Refine hypotheses / conduct additional studies.
  9. Implement control and prevention measures.
  10. Communicate findings and continue surveillance.
  • Control measures can begin before Step 9 when delay would cause preventable harm.
  • Point source: sharp single peak.
  • Continuous common source: prolonged plateau.
  • Intermittent common source: irregular repeated peaks.
  • Propagated: successive waves.
  • Epidemic threshold = observed occurrence above expected occurrence; use disease-specific DOH thresholds where available.
